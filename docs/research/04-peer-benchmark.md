## Recurring structural patterns

Most credible university research centers converge on this IA:

1. **Home** — mandate in one sentence, 3–4 pillars, featured programs/publications, latest news, clear "work with us / enroll / submit research" paths.
2. **About** — establishment, legal status, vision/mission/objectives, relationship to the parent university, governance/leadership, organizational chart.
3. **Research** — research areas/agenda, projects, publications, the journal (scope, issues, submission), data/reports.
4. **Services / Consultancy** — enumerated services with "how to commission" and contact.
5. **Training** — catalog of diplomas/courses, each with duration/audience/outcome and a registration path; calendar of upcoming intakes.
6. **Events / Conferences** — upcoming + archive, with proceedings and galleries.
7. **News** — announcements, dated, filterable.
8. **People / Team** — leadership, board of trustees, research staff, advisory network.
9. **Contact** — form, address + map, phone, email, hours, social, location within the university campus.

HRC's brief matches this well; our IA (see planning/02) follows it.

## Tone & content conventions observed

- **Formal, third‑person, restrained.** "The Center was established…", "The Center provides…".
- Vision / Mission / Objectives are almost always present verbatim as a labeled block.
- Governance transparency (named board, director) is a **trust signal** — its absence is
  noticeable. → We must chase the real names or clearly show "leadership" with roles even
  if some names are pending.
- Journals get their own mini‑site: **Aims & Scope, Editorial Board, Author Guidelines,
  Archive (issues), Ethics, Contact**. We'll model the Research page's journal section on this.
- Training pages that convert have: outcome‑first blurb, "who is this for", duration &
  format, schedule/next date, price or "subsidized", and a single clear CTA (form or WhatsApp).
- Arabic academic sites often neglect performance, SEO, and mobile polish — **that gap is
  our opportunity**: a fast, well‑structured, genuinely bilingual site will stand out.

## Feature bar to match or beat

| Feature | Typical peer | HRC target |
|---|---|---|
| Bilingual AR/EN | Often EN is thin/outdated | Full parity, AR‑first |
| Mobile experience | Frequently poor | Excellent, mobile‑first |
| Performance (Lighthouse) | 40–70 | ≥ 95 |
| SEO / structured data | Minimal | Full: metadata, OG, JSON‑LD (Organization, Course, Article, Event) |
| News / events | Static list | Dated, filterable content collection with RSS |
| Journal presence | Separate OJS | Integrated section + outbound link if OJS exists |
| Accessibility | Rarely considered | WCAG 2.2 AA |
| Contact | Email only | Form + map + WhatsApp + hours + social |

## Anti‑patterns to avoid (seen on weak peer sites)

- Wall‑of‑text About page with no hierarchy.
- Auto‑playing carousels with no controls; PDF‑only content.
- Broken English machine translation.
- Stock photos of unrelated Western campuses.
- Contact page that is just a Gmail address.
- No indication of which courses are actually open.
- Home page that buries the mandate below three screens of hero slider.
